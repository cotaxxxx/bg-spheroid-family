\begin{abstract}
A numerical search finds an equatorial saddle circle in a prolate spheroid. How can one exclude a second circle, or an off-equatorial critical point? For the squared radial--normal angle averaged against cone volume we reduce the full stationary-set diagram to six explicit computational assertions. In particular, rotation turns an entire critical orbit into a scalar root; a fourth derivative in a tangential direction determines its radial crossing. An exploratory transverse-sign cover left 21,254 of 31,340 leaves undecided. Two representations of a tangential fourth derivative give a negative upper bound on all 731 equatorial boxes for aspect ratios from two to five. A second implementation encloses the same boxes with 1,043 terminal pieces. Strict radial monotonicity then reduces the existence of a saddle circle to two endpoint signs. Over the full volume-preserving family, analytic endpoint estimates and the six named inputs yield four transitions involving the centre, axial saddle pairs, an equatorial saddle circle and boundary gradient traces.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} spheroid; stationary set; cone volume measure; radial Gauss map; Morse--Bott critical manifold; validated numerics.

\section*{Main result and status}
\noindent\textbf{Conditional classification.}
Assuming computational Assertions C1--C6, the only interior stationary
components over the entire $\lambda>0$ family are the centre, an axial pair
of index-one saddles for $\cO<\lambda<\bO$, and an equatorial
Morse--Bott saddle circle for $\bP<\lambda<\cP$.
The centre changes type at $\cO$ and $\cP$.
The full-gradient boundary trace vanishes only at the poles when
$\lambda=\bO$ and on the equatorial circle when $\lambda=\bP$.
The exact thresholds, indices and exclusions appear in
Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} and Table~\ref{tab:classification}.
The approximate thresholds are $\cO\approx0.408$, $\bO\approx0.644$,
$\bP\approx2.065$ and $\cP\approx4.724$ (Table~\ref{tab:critical}).

\noindent\textbf{Verification status.}
The analytic arguments are written out, and computational Assertions C1--C6
are stated explicitly with their source records, implementations and checking
coverage identified. This manuscript does not itself complete or reclassify
the certification of those computational inputs: the full-family
classification remains conditional on C1--C6. It is a proof-development
manuscript, not a submission-ready certification claim. In the author's
verification records (record V24, 16 November 2026;
\href{https://github.com/cotaxxxx/bg-spheroid-family/blob/127be52046e9498d1306fe3d35c3e02e91c636de/runs/2026-11-16/D_OB_GLOBAL_CLOSED_V24/JUDGE_RULING_D_OB_GLOBAL_CLOSED_20261116.md}{fixed verification record}; see
Section~\ref{sec:availability}), verification for the oblate window
$\lambda\in[2/5,33/50]$ was completed, with a separate
AI-assisted project audit of the mathematical assembly and its
computational source references, not a fresh numerical replay.
Proposition~\ref{prop:sphere} proves the
spherical case $\lambda=1$ without computational input. This editorial
revision changes neither the mathematical claims nor the status of the
computational inputs.
